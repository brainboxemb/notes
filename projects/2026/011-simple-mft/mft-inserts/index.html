MFT inserts

This page collects ideas for 20 mm MFT inserts used with the Simple MFT project. These are separate from the worktop itself: bench dogs, locating inserts, stops and other parts that plug into the dog-hole grid.

Tension-fit bench dog

A useful idea from the Mini Portable Workbench video is a simple 3D-printed bench dog that clamps itself in a 20 mm dog hole.

The visible construction is very simple:

  • nominal 20 mm cylindrical fit;
  • a small rim that rests on the worktop;
  • the lower insert section is split by two crossing slots;
  • the four resulting segments can flex radially;
  • the fit is created by the natural spring action of the printed plastic;
  • no screw, nut, O-ring or separate expansion mechanism is required.

This is attractive for the Simple MFT because it keeps the part cheap, printable and easy to replace.

Reference

  • MakerWorld — Tension Fit Bench Dogs 20mm

This appears to use the same general passive tension-fit idea as the dog shown in the Mini MFT reference video. It is useful as a design reference; the project CAD model can still be developed independently rather than copying the supplied STL.

Project CAD prototype

A first parametric prototype is being developed in the CAD library as FlexBenchDog.

Initial design values:

ItemInitial value
Nominal dog-hole diameter20.0 mm
Nominal insert diameter20.0 mm
Flared flex-tip diameter20.2 mm
Height above worktop20 mm
Insertion below worktop20 mm
Rim diameter24 mm
Rim thickness3 mm
Cross-slot width1.5 mm
Cross-slot length16 mm

The 0.2 mm tip flare is only a starting value. The main insert remains nominally 20.0 mm; only the final 2 mm at the free end flares to 20.2 mm. After the crossing slots are cut, the four free tips can spring inward in the dog hole. The useful fit will depend on the actual dog-hole diameter, printer, filament and print settings.

The CAD model uses the worktop surface as Z=0: the insert points downward and the working dog upward. The current body is 20 mm on each side of that datum. That is a modelling convention only; print orientation can be chosen independently.

The current prototype is symmetric around the worktop plane: 20 mm above and 20 mm below. It uses a light 0.6 mm chamfer around the upper edge and a larger 1.0 mm chamfer on the lower insertion edge as a lead-in. The top face has a shallow recessed square box logo.

The intended next step is to print several fit variants and compare insertion force, play and holding force.
