Filament Selection for 3D-Printed Parts

The HUB75 enclosure may need printed frame connectors, corner protectors, hinges and other fittings. The display is used outdoors once per year. The material choice is mainly about where a part sits on the same display: internal fittings support the structure, while external parts are more likely to be bumped during handling and transport.

Current direction, not a final decision: use PETG for printed parts inside the display and PCTG for parts on the outside, especially corner protectors. PCTG is considered for its impact toughness, not because it is automatically more weather-resistant. ASA is included for comparison, but is not currently needed for the intended use.

Stiffness, strength, impact resistance and flexibility

These properties describe different things:

  • Stiffness: resistance to bending. A higher flexural modulus (MPa) means less deflection for the same shape and load.
  • Strength: the maximum load or stress the material can carry before it fails.
  • Impact resistance (slagvastheid): the ability to take a sudden hit without breaking. Izod and Charpy tests measure the energy absorbed when a test specimen is struck, often reported in kJ/m².
  • Elongation at break: how far a specimen stretches before fracture. High elongation or impact resistance does not make a material rubber-like.

For example, a PLA corner may hold its shape but fracture during a sharp knock. A tougher PCTG corner may deform more before breaking. A TPU bumper is a different solution: it can deliberately compress and absorb impact energy. Impact-resistant and shock-absorbing are not interchangeable.

The printed part also depends on geometry, wall thickness, screw holes and layer orientation. Tests on different specimen shapes, standards or print directions are not directly comparable.

Stiffness comparison

The following are Bambu Lab’s own XY-direction bending-modulus results for printed specimens, not universal values for a material family.

Source: Bambu Lab Filament Guide (PDF). The PETG figure is specifically for PETG HF, not a universal PETG value. A manufacturer’s values may change with the formulation and test method.

In this dataset, PETG-CF is only about 6% stiffer than PLA, while ASA is approximately 30% less stiff than PLA. A more flexible response to bending does not automatically mean better impact resistance.

For context, Fiberlogy PCTG specifies a 1,600 MPa flexural modulus (ISO 178). It is deliberately not placed in the Bambu Lab ranking: the supplier’s test data and the Bambu printed-specimen data are not a like-for-like comparison.

Why try PCTG?

The Fiberlogy PCTG technical datasheet lists:

PropertyFiberlogy PCTGTest
Notched Izod impact strength at 23 °C92 kJ/m²ISO 180
Flexural modulus1,600 MPaISO 178
Elongation at break220%ISO 527
Heat-deflection temperature at 0.45 MPa76 °CISO 75
Heat-deflection temperature at 1.8 MPa64 °CISO 75

Fiberlogy also quotes a PETG reference range of 5–8 kJ/m² in a notched Izod test on its product page. This supports testing PCTG for parts that might be knocked or dropped. It does not mean that a printed PCTG corner will necessarily be twenty times stronger than a PETG corner. The actual geometry and print-layer bonds must be tested.

These figures are specific to Fiberlogy. A PCTG spool from another brand should not be assumed to have identical stiffness, elongation or impact resistance. PCTG is not automatically more heat-resistant or UV-resistant than other materials either.

Printing example: Fiberlogy recommends 250–270 °C nozzle, approximately 90 °C bed and drying at 60 °C for 4 hours. Follow the profile for the selected brand and check the printer’s temperature capability.

Internal versus external parts

The initial plan is one HUB75 display with two possible filament choices according to the part’s location:

Location on displayCandidateWhy
Inside the enclosurePETGPractical to print; suitable starting point for panel mounts, brackets and frame connectors that are protected by the enclosure
Outside the enclosurePCTGAttractive for corner protectors, external fittings and other pieces that may take knocks during assembly, transport or use

This is a working material selection, not yet a tested design. It does not mean all external parts must use PCTG, or that PCTG is automatically more flexible than PETG in every formulation.

Occasional outdoor use

The display will be used outside once per year, rather than permanently installed outdoors. Long-term UV ageing is therefore not the main reason to choose a filament. On this basis, there is no particular reason to switch to ASA now.

For that outdoor event, the practical questions are whether the printed parts tolerate rain, knocks during handling, and possible heat from direct sun. Pay particular attention to the internal temperature of a dark enclosure if the PETG parts carry sustained loads.

Keeping water out of the electronics is an enclosure design issue: seals, cable entries, condensation and cooling need attention regardless of whether the printed parts use PETG or PCTG.

PCTG manufacturers and buying options

PCTG is available from several brands, even though it was not identified as a Bambu Lab- or eSUN-branded product in the manufacturer catalogues checked in November 2026. Bambu Lab sells PETG variants, PETG-CF and ASA; eSUN lists PETG and PETG+HS alternatives, but these are not PCTG. Third-party PCTG can be used where the printer supports the required temperatures and settings.

Provisional choice by component

ComponentStarting materialReason / alternative
Structural frame connectorPETGUse PCTG if impact failure appears; PETG-CF only for a demonstrated stiffness need
Rigid mounting bracketPETGEvaluate screw clamping and warm-weather creep
External corner protectorPCTGPreferred candidate for impact resistance during handling and transport
Internal clip or gently flexing partPETGPCTG if impact toughness or repeated flexing warrants testing; check layer direction
Soft corner bumper or gasketTPU or dedicated sealBetter for deliberate cushioning, not a structural connector

Working recommendation: PETG inside, PCTG outside. Start with PETG for internal brackets and frame connections; print an external corner protector in PCTG (Fiberlogy as a well-documented candidate, with 3DJake or Spectrum as alternatives). Test fit, screw stress, impact and handling. The selection remains provisional until the actual parts are tested.
