Hinge Ideas

This page collects 3D-printable hinge ideas and real-world mechanism references for the HUB75 display enclosure.

The examples are starting points rather than final selections. The main questions are how far the panel must open, whether the hinge should remain hidden when closed, and how much clearance the mechanism needs around the enclosure edge.

Stop and 180-degree hinges

Stop hinge

This may be useful for a lid or support panel that must stop at a defined angle without a separate cable or support arm.

180-degree strong hinge

This is a simple reference when maximum opening angle matters more than hiding the mechanism.

180-degree opening gate hinge

The exposed mounting points make this mechanically straightforward, but it would remain visible on the outside of the enclosure.

Multi-link and concealed hinges

Multi-link hinges use several pivoting links to control both rotation and translation of a lid or panel.

That extra movement can move the panel away from the enclosure while it opens, which may help clear an overlapping edge, raised rim or seal.

Hidden hinge

The mechanism moves the panel while it rotates and therefore needs recesses and sufficient internal clearance.

180-degree concealed hinge

This is an interesting reference if the panel should open almost flat while the hinge stays hidden when closed.

Print-in-place concealed hinge

Although presented as a fidget model, it is useful as a reference for print-in-place clearances and linkage movement.

PIP hinge for interior doors

This shows how the same linkage principle can be developed from a small mechanism into a larger functional hinge.

3D-printable spider hinge

The motion could help clear an overlapping edge or seal. For a larger or more heavily loaded version, metal pins or bolts may be more suitable than printed pivots.

Real-world spider hinge references

The following commercial metal hinges are included only to understand real spider-hinge geometry, movement and construction. They are not currently proposed as parts for the display enclosure.

Osculati spider hinge

This is especially useful as a reference for pivot placement and the compact folded geometry of a real metal mechanism.

Timage slim spider hinge

The pin lock is an interesting additional idea if the enclosure lid ever needs to stay mechanically secured in its fully open position.

What to compare next

For the enclosure design, the most useful next comparison is likely:

  • simple external hinge — easiest to build and strongest, but visible;
  • concealed multi-link hinge — cleaner outside appearance, but needs more internal room;
  • spider hinge — useful when the panel must move away from an overlapping edge before rotating;
  • integrated stop or lock — useful if the lid needs a defined working/open position.
