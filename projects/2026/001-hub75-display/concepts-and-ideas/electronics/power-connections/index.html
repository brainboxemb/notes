HUB75 Power Connections

A prototype concept for distributing 5 V power from one supply to the HUB75 LED matrix panels.

Proposed concept

The prototype is deliberately simple:

  • 1 × removable 20 A input for the main 5 V supply;
  • 2 × 4-pole barrier-strip terminals for the HUB75 panel outputs;
  • 1 × additional 2-pole 10 A screw terminal for PCB / auxiliary 5 V;
  • all mounted on a 4 × 6 cm double-sided experiment PCB.

The two 4-pole barrier strips provide eight screw positions in total. Five positions are intended for the five HUB75 panel feeds, leaving three spare positions.

The removable 20 A connector is only the main input. The PCB-side connector is the 90° male part, so the supply cable plugs in from the edge of the board. The matching female screw-terminal plug stays attached to the power-supply cable.

Electrical construction

The experiment PCB is mainly a convenient mechanical carrier. The small copper pads and plated holes should not be used as the main high-current distribution path.

A practical prototype would use two substantial conductors on the underside:

  • one continuous +5 V bus;
  • one continuous GND bus.

Each pole of the two barrier strips then gets a short connection to the appropriate power bus. Thick tinned copper wire or another suitably dimensioned bus conductor is preferable to trying to carry the combined panel current through the small prototyping-board pads.

The input connector, bus conductors and supply wiring must all be dimensioned for the combined current of the complete display, not just the current of one panel.

Mechanical checks before building

There is one important point to verify before ordering the complete set.

The 4 × 6 cm experiment PCB has 1 mm round holes. The TinyTronics 4-pole 7.62 mm barrier terminal uses 1.1 mm rectangular pins and TinyTronics explicitly marks that connector as not experiment-PCB friendly. The 20 A PCB-side input connector uses 1.0 mm square pins.

So the 2.54 mm / 7.62 mm pitch alignment is convenient, but the physical pin fit still needs to be checked. Possible solutions are:

  • carefully enlarging the required prototype-board holes;
  • selecting a prototype board with slightly larger holes;
  • or moving directly to a small custom PCB after confirming the layout.

The first prototype should also confirm that the complete 2 × 4-pole barrier-strip arrangement plus input and auxiliary connector physically fits on 60 × 40 mm with enough room for cable lugs and screwdriver access.
