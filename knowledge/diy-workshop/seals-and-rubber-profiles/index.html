Seals, Gaskets & Rubber Profiles

Rubber and flexible strips can be useful in two ways: keeping rain out of an enclosure and protecting edges or surfaces from bumps and scratches. Some profiles can help with both, depending on how they are fitted.

Common options

Material or productUseful forPractical detail
EPDM rubber or foamSealing a lid, back panel or other jointOften used as a compressible strip between two surfaces
Silicone rubberSeals and flexible profilesAvailable in strips, cords and different shapes
Flexible PVCStootranden, edge protection and removable inserts (pezen)Interesting when you want a soft strip inside a rigid holder
TPU (3D printed)Custom bumpers and flexible partsEasy to combine with a printed holder; printed seals need some experimentation
Adhesive sealing tapeCovering a fixed seamUseful when the enclosure will not be opened at that joint

A lid seal works by being squeezed between two surfaces. An edge protector is mainly there to take knocks. They may look similar, but are fitted differently.

3M: two references

  • 3M Gasket Attachment — ways of attaching a rubber or foam gasket to a panel or cover.
  • 3M Extreme Sealing Tape 4411 / 4412 — sealing tape for fixed joints and seams.

These are useful starting points to look at the different ways of sealing a joint.

Interesting combination with 3D printing

Instead of printing an entire flexible edge, print a holder for a ready-made PVC insert. That way the holder determines the shape and the softer insert makes contact with other surfaces.

See the PVC 180 insert idea for a concrete example with a supplier photo.

For a practical build, first check the profile’s dimensions and how it is held in place, then try a small test piece.
