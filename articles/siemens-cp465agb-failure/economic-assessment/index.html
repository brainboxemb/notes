Economic assessment: repair or replace?

Useful life, estimated original retail price, depreciation, warranty and repair-versus-replacement economics.

Updated: 01 Nov 2026

Economic value: expected lifespan and depreciation

The remaining useful life matters when weighing a repair against replacement. A device that is worth repairing at three years old may be a less attractive repair at eight years old, especially if the repair is expensive.

How long should a combination microwave oven last?

There is no reliable model-specific lifespan figure for the CP465AGB0/03. For context:

  • The European Environment Agency reports an observed average life of about 9 years and 10 months for microwave ovens, based on consumer survey data from four European countries. This is not a measurement of Siemens built-in combi ovens specifically.
  • Consumer NZ estimates 8 years for countertop microwaves and 13–15 years for ovens and stoves. A combined appliance cannot automatically be assigned either figure.
  • A Dutch kitchen-appliance overview suggests 10–12 years for built-in combination ovens/microwaves. This is a general guide, not failure-rate data for this model.

For a working assumption, use 10–12 years for the combination microwave/oven and 15 years as an optimistic scenario, not a promised lifespan. Usage, ventilation, maintenance and individual component failures can move the outcome substantially.

The FD number 9902 dates manufacture to February 2019. As of November 2026, the appliance is approximately 7 years and 9 months old (7.75 years). The actual purchase/installation date is not known.

Estimate the original retail price, not just the list price

The 2020 Siemens studioLine catalogue lists €1,499 for the CP465AGB0 (the 2021/22 catalogue lists €1,599). These are recommended catalogue prices, not necessarily the actual amounts paid.

A contemporary example shows why a discount is reasonable: Siemens lists the regular iQ500 CM585AGS1 at a €1,249 recommended price, while Siemens itself offers it for €744 (60% of list) and Coolblue has advertised it for €619 (50% of list). These are two current examples for a different model, not evidence of the precise discount on the CP465AGB0 when it was purchased.

For the historical CP465AGB0, 60% of the 2020 list price is a useful illustrative retail-price assumption: €1,499 × 60% ≈ €900. A plausible sensitivity range to test is 50–70% of list (€750–€1,050). The actual purchase price and installation date are still unknown.

Depreciation: a bookkeeping scenario, not resale value

Linear depreciation is useful as a simple calculation, but it does not represent actual second-hand market prices. Appliances generally lose value quickly once used, and remaining resale value depends on condition, demand and warranties. No verified second-hand valuation for this exact model is available. The following table is only a hypothetical book value for a working oven.

Assume the oven was put into service around its February 2019 manufacturing date: approximately 7.75 years have elapsed as of November 2026. Using €900 as the estimated initial retail price, straight-line depreciation to zero gives:

At the 12-year assumption, varying the original retail price from 50% to 70% of the €1,499 catalogue price gives an illustrative remaining value of roughly €265–€372. Formula: working book value = estimated paid price × max(0, 1 − age ÷ assumed lifespan).

These are accounting-style estimates for a working oven, not resale values. The oven is currently defective, so its as-is market value is unknown and likely substantially lower. A low book value alone does not prove that a repair is uneconomical: the next years of usable service matter more than the sunk original purchase cost.

Siemens repair costs: the €149 starting charge changes the calculation

A Siemens out-of-warranty repair starts at €149 even when no replacement part is needed. This is a substantial fixed cost, but its percentage of the replacement price cannot yet be calculated: the intended replacement is the Siemens studioLine CM485AGB1 without steam, and a current dealer selling price is still unknown. What is a new warranty worth?

A new Siemens oven generally comes with a two-year manufacturer’s warranty, subject to the applicable conditions. This offers more certainty about covered repair costs in the first two years. A repair does not renew the warranty on all the other components of a nearly eight-year-old appliance. See Siemens’ warranty information.

Under Dutch consumer law, statutory conformity rights are separate from a manufacturer’s guarantee and are not automatically limited to two years; what a buyer may reasonably expect of the product matters. The seller is the first point of contact. See ACM ConsuWijzer. The original purchase date and seller are still unknown, so no free remedy can be assumed.

Warranty has economic value as reduced risk, but without model-specific fault probabilities and terms it is not justified to assign it an arbitrary €100 or €200 cash amount.

Comparing future years of use

The relevant replacement is a studioLine CM485AGB1 without steam, as requested. Unlike a non-studioLine model, its dealer price is on request, and a current selling price has not yet been established. The €620–€660 prices of the regular iQ500 are useful as general market context only; they must not be used to conclude that replacing this studioLine oven is cheaper than repairing it.

For the existing oven, focus on the complete repair bill and how many additional years of reliable use it could provide. A hypothetical €323.50 all-in repair corresponds to about €162 per additional year at two more years, or €81 per year at four more years. Neither lifespan can be predicted from the current symptoms.

The new studioLine would have a fresh appliance warranty and a new expected service life; a repaired old oven retains its ageing components. These advantages are real but cannot be priced precisely without reliability data.

Decision still open: obtain a real all-in dealer quotation for the CM485AGB1, including any installation, and compare it with Siemens’ repair price (minimum €149, then any parts and labour) and the warranty terms. There is no defensible financial recommendation to replace the oven based solely on the cheaper regular iQ500 price.
